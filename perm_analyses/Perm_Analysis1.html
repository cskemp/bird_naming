How the tests work

The outcome is the species × species matrix of SameName. It is never changed, so all the dependence between pairs is kept: each species appears in many pairs, and if A and B share a name and B and C do, then A and C must too.

To test whether a measure X adds to a set of measures Z (Z may be empty):

  1. Fit SameName ~ Z + X and SameName ~ Z by logistic regression. The statistic is the signed root deviance gain, s = sign(−βX) · √(2 · ΔLL). It is positive when more distant birds are less likely to share a name, which is the expected direction.
  2. Regress X on Z over the pairs, and keep the residual matrix.
  3. Relabel the species of the residual matrix at random, refit, and recompute
    1. This was repeated 2000 times.

This is MRQAP with double-semi-partialling (Dekker, Krackhardt & Snijders, 2007). With Z empty it is plain QAP, which is the same as shuffling species among the folk categories while keeping their sizes fixed.

For the non-nested comparisons (74 vs 23, Per vs Phy), each measure is tested given the other in both directions. If 74 adds to 23 but 23 adds nothing to 74, then 74 is the better measure. If each adds to the other, then each carries something the other lacks, and the test doesn’t pick a winner.

“All” is a pooled model with a fixed intercept for each language and common slopes. Species are relabelled within each language.

Calibration

Before the results: does the test have the right false-positive rate? For each language, 100 “noise” predictors were made by shuffling the species labels of the Per matrix. Each one has the matrix structure of a real distance measure but no true relation to naming. Each was tested given 74, by MRQAP and by the naive Wald test from the logistic regression, which treats pairs as independent, as BIC does.

The naive test treats thousands of dependent pairs as independent, so it finds “effects” of pure noise far more often than 5% of the time. This is issue A1 in REVIEW.md in concrete form. The BIC comparisons in the paper have the same problem.

1974 vs 2023: category bootstrap

The encompassing tests above ask whether each taxonomy carries information the other lacks. They don’t give an effect size for “how much better is 1974?” with uncertainty that reflects the category structure. For that, the folk categories were resampled with replacement within each language, and LL(74) − LL(23) was recomputed on each resample (2000 resamples). Large categories contribute many same-name pairs, so the result depends heavily on which of them are drawn. The interval shows how much. The differences are rescaled to the original number of pairs.

Summary

These notes describe the full run (B = 2000). They are written by hand, so re-check them if the settings change.

  • Calibration. MRQAP’s false-positive rate is close to nominal. The naive test’s rate is about 4–5 times too high.
  • (a) Every measure predicts name-sharing in every language (p < .001).
  • Per adds to 74 in five languages and in the pooled model. It does not in Innu or Saami.
  • Phy never adds to 74 in the expected direction. Given 74, or 74 + Per, its slope has a significantly wrong sign in several languages and in the pooled model. So the wrong-sign coefficients (A2) are not just noise from collinearity: once taxonomy is controlled, birds that are phylogenetically closer are somewhat less likely to share a name.
  • 74 adds to Phy + Per in every language.
  • 74 vs 23. 74 adds to 23 in all seven languages. But 23 also adds to 74 (expected sign) in Tzeltal, Zapotec, Tobelo and the pooled model, so each taxonomy carries something the other lacks. In the category bootstrap, the 95% interval for LL(74) − LL(23) excludes 0 only in Tlingit. Pooled, 90% of resamples favour 74, but the interval includes 0. Most languages lean towards 1974, but how big its advantage is depends on a few large categories. The data don’t support the claim that 1974 is decisively better.

Limitations

  • The null is “relabel species”. MRQAP asks whether the observed match between a measure’s residual and the folk names is better than for random relabellings of the same residual. It keeps the dependence in the outcome exactly, but the relabelled residuals don’t keep the clade structure of a real residual (e.g. 74 and 23 differ in a few specific clades). The calibration check suggests the test’s error rate is close to nominal for a predictor with realistic structure.
  • The bootstrap is approximate. Duplicated categories bring duplicated between-category pairs, and different copies of the same category can’t be paired. Each overdifferentiated species is resampled with its smallest category, and keeps its other memberships through the original SameName values (9 memberships in total; 5 in Tobelo).
  • Seven languages. The pooled tests treat the seven languages as fixed. Whether a result carries over to other folk taxonomies is the question that leave-one-language-out in cv_analyses/ addresses, with little power.
